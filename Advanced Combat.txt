Below are my mostly organized design notes for the advanced combat system

# BLOOD
Blood is a pool that actors have that can be drained through wounds acquired in combat.  At certain volumes, debuffs are applied,

100% would be full supply and no debuffs.  
80% would be light effects (-1 all stats)
60% would be strong effects (-2 all stats, 20% movement AP reduction)
40% would be severe effects (-4 all stats, 40% movement AP reduction, -20% universal AP reduction)
20% remaining would be unconsciousness (actor cannot act until blood recovers to at least 40%)
0% blood remaining would be death

To be clear this isn't actually 0% blood remaining, this is just when the amount of blood left would cause death.
# LIMBS
The attacker makes a roll against the defenders AC
If the AC roll is passed then another d20 is rolled to determine which body part is hit

1 - 11 = random limb hit 25% chance each
12 - 18 = torso hit
19 - 20 = head hit

then the damage rolls happen as normal

# WOUNDS
then the wound system comes into play

if an attack does less than 15% CURRENT HP of limb = no wound
15 - 30% of CURRENT HP = light wound (10% of total blood volume per hour)
30 - 90% = moderate wound (30% of total blood volume per hour)
90 - 100% = critical wound (50% of total blood volume per hour)

wounds can be applied to any body part and have the same effects for each
these are just sharp weapon damage, working on blunt damage later

# CRITICAL DEBUFFS
The critical system will result in effects scaling with the multiplier and lasting for a short period.  The intent is that they are short term shock effects caused by hitting critical areas (think of kidney shot from WoW) 

arms
2x = 10% Universal AP generation decrease
3x = 20% AP generation decrease
4x = 30% AP generation decrease

legs
2x = 20% Movement AP generation decrease
3x = 40% AP generation decrease
4x = 60% AP generation decrease

torso
2x = 25% bleed rate, -10% Universal AP generation decrease, and -2 constitution
3x = 50% bleed rate, -20% AP generation decrease, and -4 constitution
4x = 75% bleed rate, -30% AP generation decrease, and -6 constitution

head
head crits would apply the x2/x3/x4 multiplier directly to incoming damage just like a normal crit.  the head itself will have less HP than other parts and be easier to destroy.
additionally, head crits will apply the torso debuff at 1/2 magnitude.  so a 2x head crit will multiply the damage by x2 AND apply a 12% bleed, 5% AP gen decrease, and -1 constitution debuff.

# OVERKILL
Dealing 200% of a body parts current health should overkill it

Overkilled arms/legs are severed for a -50% Universal/movement AP generation.  This is for a humanoid, we should implement it so that it scales with the # of arms legs, i.e. a 4 legged creature should suffer a 25% reduction of movement AP if one leg is severed.

Overkilled torsos apply the 4x crit debuff.  If the debuff is already on the target, double its effects (150% bleed rate, 60% AP decrease, -12 constitution.)  A torso at 0 health is overkilled by any attack that deals >0 damage.

Overkilled head means no head which means dead

## SEVERING/MANGLING
Overkilled limbs are severed by sharp weapons.  Equipment on those limbs (rings/weapons/shields) are dropped with the limb.
Limbs that are brought to 0 without being severed are mangled, applying the same debuffs as severed but with no loss of the limb (meaning it can be easily healed)

Both states result in attack rolls that target that limb being redirected to the torso instead.
# BODY PART HEALTH
All of this math is referenced from Pathfinder 1e stats and was just so I could have a baseline reference.  The only thing currently implemented here is the body part health ratios per HP.
HP = (level - 1 * (class specific LP bonus)) + (LP * (con bonus)) + (class specific LP bonus * 2)

torso = 100% of health
limbs = 70% of health
head = 50% of health

so HP is a bookkeeping stat used in the background to determine body part health
class specific LP bonuses are the averages of the hit dice used for that class.  d10s round up, all others round down.
so a d6 becomes +3, a d8 becomes +4, a d10 becomes +6

level 7 warrior with +5 con bonus
HP = (6 * 6) + (7 * 5) + (6 * 2) = 83

torso HP = 100% of HP = 83
limbs = 70% of HP = 58
head = 50% of HP = 41

level 7 wizard with +2 con
HP = (6 * 3) + (7 * 2) + (3 * 2) = 38

torso = 38
limbs = 26
head = 19

So for example the player starts with 100 health + 50 from an equipped amulet of heath.  So their,
torso has 150 health
arms/leges have 105 health (70% of 150)
head has 75 health (50% of 150)

taking off the amulet adjusts the body part health accordingly
torso = 100
arm/legs = 70
head = 50
## DEATH

# COMBAT RATING
also here's my dynamic threat assessment thing COMBAT RATING
COMBAT RATING
*HIT DICE = LEVEL POINTS (LP)

Combat Rating (CR) should reflect the combined potential strength of that character at that level while factoring in their current body part health, AP generation, blood pool levels as % of total, actual attack rating and damage (to account for physical potential), and actual mana levels (to account for magical potential)
base CR should be equal to LP (level Points, specific term translated from pathfinders Hit Dice, same thing basically in its function as a universal indicator of level) 
of the entity modified by the difference between the unbuffed (magical buffs or gear), undamaged (no wounds), fully rested (no fatigue or ailments) theoretical max of these values compared to their actual values.
to shorten this, baseline (inherent) potential vs actual (current) potential
baseline potential would include attribute bonuses but only PERMANENT bonuses, like those from character creation, actual attribute increases from leveling, or permanent buffs like tomes/potions. 
Permanent here means a few things; that the buff has an indefinite duration and that it is not able to be dispelled or removed in any way other than extremely specific methods (gear buffs are technically permanent and not able to be dispelled, this accounts for that).
baseline potential is a CONSTANT and only changes on level up or unique situations like reading a stat tome or drinking a stat potion.

all other buffs come from external sources (casted buffs, gear buffs, ability/perk buffs etc...) and should be counted as actual potential modifiers.  debuffs are also actual potential modifiers.

MAIN FORMULA
--------------
Actual Modifier = Body Part Health * AP generation * Blood Volume * Attack/Damage rating * Mana levels
Actual CR = Base CR * Actual Modifier

modifier sum is clamped to 0.25 and 2, so it either cuts CR into 1/4 or doubles it max.

*ALL MODIFIER RESULTS ARE CLAMPED FROM 0.5 to 2

Body part health
-----------------
body parts will be "in danger" at 80% or less of total part HP
having 1 in danger limb with result in this modifier = 0.9
-2 = 0.8 etc..., bottoming out at 0.5
having all limbs in danger will result in this modifier = 0.5

modifier = 1 - ((# of parts in danger) * 0.1)

AP generation
-------------
ratio of actual AP generation vs baseline of 100

200 / 100 = 2
100 / 100 = 1
50 / 100 = 0.5

modifier = (current AP gen) / 100

blood volume
-------------
current % of total vs total

100% = 1
40% = 0.5

modifier = 0.5 + ((((current % / 100) - 0.4)/0.6) * 0.5)

mana volume
------------
100% = 1
40% = 0.5

modifier = 0.5 + ((((current % / 100) - 0.4)/0.6) * 0.5)

attack/damage rating
---------------------
ratio of baseline sums vs actual sums
Current_Combat / Baseline_Combat

Baseline Combat = Baseline_Attack + (Avg_Dice_Damage * Num_Attacks) + Baseline_Damage_Bonus
Current Combat  = Current_Attack + (Avg_Dice_Damage * Num_Attacks) + Current_Damage_Bonus

so if BC = 10 and CC = 20, then this modifer = 2
if BC == CC, then = 1
if BC = 10 and CC = 5, then this modifier = 0.5

modifier = Current_Combat / Baseline_Combat

# ADDITIONS/ADJUSTMENTS/THOUGHTS AFTER IMPLEMENTATION
## SHARP VS BLUNT DAMAGE
The current implementation of the system basically is the sharp system, with wounds applying bleed and severing being possible.

Blunt damage should not apply bleed wounds and should not be capable of severing limbs.
Blunt wounds should apply direct AP generation reduction

15 - 30% of CURRENT HP = light wound (10% of total blood volume per hour)
30 - 90% = moderate wound (30% of total blood volume per hour)
90 - 100% = critical wound (50% of total blood volume per hour)

## CRIT DEBUFF REORIENTATION
Re-working these into more of a "critical location hit", the inspiration being kidney shot from various RGPs (mostly WoW)

## DEATH AND EXPERIENCE
there are only two ways for an entity to die; 0 head health or 0 effective constitution.

0 head health is by design reliant on rare dice rolls.
0 effective constitution should be the way most entities die.  Currently this should be consistently achievable by attacking a 0 health torso, which should apply the 4x crit debuff and its -6 constitution penalty each hit.
Limbs will be hit the most, but severing/mangling a limb will remove it from the target list and redirect those rolls to the torso.  Later on we will implement toggles/abilities/traits that widen certain body part windows, i.e. allow the entity to focus attacks on a specific body part.

Experience will eventually be refactored entirely into a new class system.  The idea is to gain experience primarily through ACTION (so fighting with a melee weapon would increase "fighter" exp or something, I'm still designing it lol, ranged would level "archer", magic would level "mage" etc...)
